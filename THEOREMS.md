## Abstract
We study isomorphism of induced complements of subcubes and generalized Fibonacci cubes with forbidden words of length k in dimension k+r. Under k>=r+3, rigidity reduces isomorphism to signed coordinate permutations. Separated occurrences admit a classification by r-Abelian equivalence of consecutive bit differences, up to reversal. For arbitrary overlaps, signed subcube representations and active-coordinate compression yield an exact reduction to hypergraph isomorphism, with theoretical word-RAM time O(kr+2^{O(r)}). The r=1 classification and unrestricted sharpness boundary are known; the conjecture results here are restricted. The external isomorphism algorithm is not implemented or benchmarked.

## 1. Definitions and rigidity
Let Q_d have vertex set {0,1}^d and edges at Hamming distance one. A subcube is specified by a word over {0,1,*}, with stars free. Its codimension is its number of fixed coordinates. Let G be the induced complement of a union of m such subcubes.

Lemma 1. If each subcube fixes at least m+1 coordinates, G is nonempty and connected.
Proof. Write L_i for the set of fixed coordinates of forbidden cube i and b_i(j) for its prescribed bit at j in L_i. A witness at a surviving vertex x is a coordinate w_i in L_i with x[w_i]=1-b_i(w_i). A witness assignment w=(w_1,...,w_m) is compatible if all witness bit requirements agree on repeated coordinates. Its safe subcube consists of all vertices satisfying these requirements. Distinct witness coordinates are automatically compatible. Greedy selection of distinct coordinates, followed by assignment of the required bits, proves nonemptiness.

Starting at any surviving vertex x, choose a witness for every cube. To replace witness w_i by a coordinate t in L_i not currently used by any witness, change the vertex bit at t to 1-b_i(t), if needed, while retaining the old witnesses; then designate t as the witness for cube i. At most m coordinates are occupied, so such t exists. Every actual vertex change is a safe edge. Processing each witness once in this way yields distinct witness coordinates, since each newly chosen coordinate was unused and subsequent replacements cannot choose an occupied coordinate.

To connect two surviving vertices, first obtain distinct witness assignments from both by safe paths. Transform the first assignment to the second by settling target coordinates one at a time. If a desired target t is already the current witness of that same cube, it is already settled. Otherwise, if t is occupied by another cube, that occupant is not settled (the final target coordinates are distinct). Move the occupant to a coordinate in its own L_i unused by the current assignment. There are at most m occupied coordinates in total, or m-1 belonging to other cubes; with |L_i|>=m+1 an unused coordinate exists even after excluding its own current coordinate. Now t is unused and the desired witness can be assigned there by the safe operation above. This does not change any settled coordinate. After all targets are settled, the current vertex and the second path's endpoint satisfy the same distinct witness requirements, so they are connected inside their safe subcube by changing only unconstrained bits. Reverse the second path to reach the original second vertex. QED.

Theorem 2. For two induced complements in Q_d, each obtained by deleting at most m subcubes of codimension at least m+2, every graph isomorphism extends uniquely to a signed coordinate permutation of Q_d.
Proof. For direction j, identify its edges in the surviving graph with their projections to Q_{d-1}. A j-edge survives exactly when its projection avoids the projections of all deleted subcubes. Each projected deletion has codimension at least m+1. Lemma 1 implies that the edge-base graph is nonempty and connected. Adjacent bases correspond to opposite j-edges of a surviving square. Thus the equivalence classes generated by opposite edges of 4-cycles are precisely the d coordinate directions: no 4-cycle can equate two different directions. Every isomorphism permutes these intrinsic classes. Fix a surviving vertex x and its image y. The signed coordinate permutation with that direction permutation sending x to y agrees with the isomorphism on every edge, hence on the connected surviving graph. Since all directions occur and x survives, the extension is unique. QED.

## 2. Separated occurrence classification
Let f be a binary word of length k, d=k+r, r>=1 and k>=r+3. Let C_i, 0<=i<=r, be the cube requiring f at offset i. Let S_f be their union. Theorem 2 applies with m=r+1.

Theorem 3. Let |f|=|g|=k with the same d=k+r, r>=1 and k>=r+3. If both f and g have at least two mismatches between their prefix and suffix overlaps at every shift 1,...,r, then Q_d(f) and Q_d(g) are isomorphic iff Delta(f) and Delta(g) are r-Abelian equivalent, up to reversal of one word. Delta(f)_j=f_j XOR f_{j+1}.
Proof. The mismatch assumption makes all occurrence cubes pairwise distance >=2, hence exactly the connected components of the deleted graph. A signed coordinate permutation must permute these cubes. Their free sets F_i satisfy |F_i intersect F_j|=r-|i-j|. Consequently the induced permutation of offsets preserves their distances and is identity or reversal. Coordinate reversal of the ambient cube replaces g by reverse(g), so it suffices to compare aligned offsets. Write the r+1 occurrence patterns as rows. A signed coordinate permutation preserving row order exists iff the multisets of column patterns agree modulo independent bit complementation. A column in either boundary (first r or last r coordinates) has a unique star mask, with 1 through r fixed entries. Modulo bit complement its fixed entries are determined by consecutive XORs, which are precisely the prefixes or suffixes of Delta(f) of lengths 0 through r-1, in reverse order as appropriate. The k-r interior columns have no stars and their consecutive XORs are precisely all length-r factors of Delta(f), in reverse order. Thus column multiset equality is exactly equality of the length-r factor counts and the length-(r-1) prefix and suffix. By Karhumaki–Saarela–Zamboni [KSZ], arXiv v1, Lemma 2.3, these data characterize r-Abelian equivalence. Here the length k-1 is at least r-1. QED.

Prior result: for r=1, the criterion simply counts bit changes and is already covered, without the separation restriction, by Azarija et al. [AKLPR], arXiv v1, Theorem 3.1 (p. 4). No novelty is claimed for that case.

Corollaries under the same separation assumption: (i) isomorphism descends to d-1, by the same criterion with r-1 when r>=2; when r=1 all single-point deletions are isomorphic; (ii) nontrivial isomorphism requires k>=2r+2 = the inequality k>=2(d+1)/3: distinct r-Abelian words of length <=2r-1 cannot occur, and distinct equivalent words of length 2r are reversals ([KSZ], arXiv v1, Lemma 2.4 and Theorem 2.6); equality of Delta up to reversal gives equality of original words up to reversal and complement. (iii) the number of runs is 1 plus the number of ones in Delta, and is preserved. These are restricted results related to Azarija et al., Conjectures 2.3, 2.4, 3.2 in arXiv v1; not full solutions.

Sharp family: f=0^(r+1)1^r0 and g=0^r1^(r+1)0, r>=2. Each shift h<=r crosses the initial transition in at least one pair and the terminal transition in another pair. Their difference words are 0^r 1 0^(r-1) 1 and 0^(r-1) 1 0^r 1. Each has the same boundary factors of length r-1; the first 1 lies in r length-r windows, once at each position, the terminal 1 in one more window at its final position, and exactly one window is all zeros. Thus they are r-Abelian equivalent. The words are not related by reversal/complement and have k=2r+2, d=3r+2. This establishes sharpness only within the separated class. The boundary was already attained in the unrestricted existence problem by [AKLPR], Theorem 2.2 (arXiv v1, p. 3), using two-block words. No new unrestricted sharpness claim is made.

## 3. Arbitrary overlaps: signed subcube representation
For k>=r, let P={h in [1,r]: f[0:k-h]=f[h:k]} and D=P\(P+P). Then

1_{S_f}=sum_{i=0}^r 1_{C_i} - sum_{h in D} sum_{i=0}^{r-h} 1_{C_i intersect C_{i+h}}.

Proof. For i<j, the intersection is nonempty iff j-i is a period. For any i<t<j, the fixed interval of C_t lies inside the union of the two endpoint intervals; their intersection either lies entirely in C_t or is disjoint from it. In the nonempty endpoint intersection, containment in C_t is equivalent to t-i and j-t both being periods: the pairwise prescriptions then agree everywhere. Thus the retained pairs are exactly consecutive occurrences at any particular point x. With q occurrences, there are q-1 retained pairs for q>0, none for q=0. This proves the identity pointwise. The number of terms is <=(r+1)(r+2)/2, and summing gives |S_f|=(r+1)2^r - sum_{h in D}(r+1-h)2^(r-h). The counting formula is not claimed novel; autocorrelation approaches are known.

## 4. Active coordinates and exact compression
Call a coordinate active if an edge of S_f occurs in that direction; write a for their number and c for the number of components of S_f. Every free coordinate of an occurrence cube is active. Their union has size 2r. Build the graph on occurrence offsets with edges whenever two cubes are at distance <=1. The distance is the mismatch count on their common fixed positions. Components of this offset graph correspond exactly to components of S_f; active coordinates are the free ones plus unique mismatch coordinates of pairs at distance one.

Theorem 4. a+c<=3r+1. Proof: project onto the coordinates outside the union of free sets. Each occurrence cube becomes one fixed string there. Within each component, choose one representative occurrence and span the differences of all other occurrences in that component to its representative. Across components there are at most r+1-c generators. Every active direction outside the free sets gives a unit vector in this span, from an actual adjacent pair of vertices in one component. Such unit vectors are independent, so there are at most r+1-c of them. Add 2r. QED. Also the affine dimension of S_f is <=3r, by the 2r free directions plus r representative differences.

Let U_j be the projection of component j onto all active coordinates. Every inactive coordinate is constant on a component. Record its vector of values across the c components modulo complement, with multiplicity. An ambient signed permutation mapping S_f to S_g exists iff there is a permutation of components and a signed permutation of active coordinates mapping the projected sets U_j accordingly, and carrying the inactive pattern multiplicity function to the other one. Necessity follows from preservation of edges, components and coordinates. Sufficiency follows by matching inactive coordinates with equal normalized patterns, flipping each to its matching orientation; together with the active map this maps each full component exactly. No requirement of disjointness between the projected U_j is needed.

## 5. Single-exponential theoretical bound
For I subset of active coordinates define W_j(I)=sum_{x in U_j}(-1)^(sum_{i in I}x_i). Project the signed terms in section 3 within their own component. Projection is injective there because inactive coordinates are constant, and each term is contained in one component. A projected cube with free set F contributes zero if I intersects F; otherwise it contributes 2^|F| times (-1)^(sum of its fixed bits in I). Sum with signs. Fourier inversion makes this data lossless. No enumeration of forbidden vertices is needed for this computation; there is still exponential coefficient enumeration.

Encode a colored hypergraph with 2a literal vertices (i,0),(i,1), and c component vertices. Distinct vertex types are enforced. Pair hyperedges link the two literals of each coordinate. For each nonzero W_j(I) and each assignment b:I->{0,1}, include a hyperedge consisting of component j and the literals (i,b_i), colored by the signed integer W_j(I)(-1)^(sum b_i), with Fourier tag. Omission encodes zero. A bit flip swaps the appropriate literals and multiplies the Fourier coefficient by the same parity, so this encoding is equivariant; conversely a type- and pair-preserving map forces exactly the Fourier transformation law, and Fourier inversion gives U_j equality. For each complementary class of inactive component patterns {B,B^c}, encode both subsets B and B^c as hyperedges of a separate type colored by their common multiplicity (omit zero). Since c>=1, the two subsets are distinct; the empty subset is permitted in this intermediate structure. This encoding enforces exactly the criterion in section 4.

To apply the uncolored hypergraph theorem, rank the distinct edge/vertex labels jointly for both inputs; there are 2^O(r) labels, so O(r) bits suffice. Add a rigid anchor path of O(r) vertices, whose consecutive pairs are the only 2-edges in the final structure, and whose first vertex is the only singleton edge. Give the path at least three vertices. These features identify the anchors and pin their order. Encode every original colored edge as its original subset union the same three fixed anchors and a subset of the remaining anchors coding its type/label. Encode each original vertex color likewise by an augmented singleton of that vertex and a separate type/label. All such data edges have size >=3; no collisions occur between distinct tagged data, and the original vertices are exactly the nonanchors. Empty original subsets also encode without ambiguity. Thus ordinary hypergraph isomorphism is equivalent to the colored relation. Total vertices O(r) by Theorem 4. The hypergraph isomorphism algorithm of Luks [L] has running time 2^{O(n)} m^{O(1)} for n vertices and m hyperedges, as stated explicitly by Arvind and Vasudev [AV], Section 1. Here n=O(r) and m=2^{O(r)}, including the anchor and label edges. Hence the running time is 2^{O(r)} (the polynomial factor in m is absorbed). The reduction uses Luks as an external result; direct publisher access to [L] was unavailable.

Preprocessing: scan the overlaps for every h<=r in O(kr) time, retaining the mismatch count and position if unique. All pairs use the same offset-specific overlap, and can be processed in O(r^2) further time. Collect active coordinates and component representatives; for each inactive coordinate read the c representative bits, normalize and count by a binary trie in O(dc)=O(kr). Construct the projected signed terms by random access to f, in poly(r) operations; compute all coefficients and the labeled hyperedges in 2^O(r) time, including label ranking and poly(r) bit arithmetic for coefficients. Counts up to d fit the word-RAM word (at least log d bits). The complete theoretical time is O(kr+2^O(r)) on this model. In particular, fixed r yields linear dependence on k. Luks is invoked as an existing theorem, not implemented or benchmarked in this package. No claim of polynomial dependence on r or practical speedup for large r.

## 6. Verification and references
Run `python verify.py` and `python verify_rigidity.py` (the latter needs NetworkX). First script checks every word for 1<=r<=4, r+3<=k<=8 against direct membership, components, active coordinates, separated column signature, selected Fourier coefficients, and the sharp family r=2,...,100. Second checks random general deletion unions and stratified residual graph pairs versus full forbidden-point incidence and compressed Fourier incidence. NetworkX is a finite testing backend, not Luks. The supplied logs are from the v0.7 Linux author-side runs. The unchanged scripts also have a matching Windows console transcript reported by the author. The rigidity script compares 42 pairs (19 isomorphic, 23 nonisomorphic); the (k,r)=(5,2) group contains no nontrivial isomorphic pair. The independent AI reviewer reported separate nauty-based comparisons, including nontrivial pairs, but the reviewer code and raw logs were not supplied here. The reported independent audit is evidence distinct from these finite author-side tests. Neither finite testing nor AI audit constitutes human peer review or a substitute for proof.
